---
title: "The signal chain: how sound is prepared for recognition"
---

Before Whisper, each speech fragment goes through ffmpeg:

```
-af "highpass=f=80,lowpass=f=6000,afftdn=nf=-20,acompressor=threshold=-25dB:ratio=4:attack=5:release=50,loudnorm" \
-ac 1 -ar 16000
```

- **highpass=80** cuts hum, vibration and excess low end
- **lowpass=6000** removes high-frequency hiss
- **afftdn** suppresses constant background noise
- **acompressor** evens out quiet and loud phrases
- **ratio=4** compresses peaks at roughly 4:1
- **attack=5** reacts quickly to sharp sounds
- **release=50** brings the level back smoothly
- **loudnorm** normalises the final loudness
- **mono**, **16 kHz** bring the recording to Whisper's format

It's easy to overdo it here.

Heavy noise reduction starts removing word endings along with the background. Compression can raise the noise, and a noise gate can cut off quiet speech.

So the main mode cleans the recording but tries not to damage the voice.

For difficult conditions there's a harsher Noisy preset.

As a result Whisper receives not the original iPhone recording but a prepared signal with more even loudness and less background noise.
