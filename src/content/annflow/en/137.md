---
title: "Automating applications as an answer to market asymmetry"
---

I didn't start with a platform or an elegant architecture.

I was simply tired of the humiliating mechanics of looking for work on HH.

You open a vacancy.
You read the description.
You try to work out whether it's even alive or just hanging there as a display.
You pick a resume.
You write a cover letter.
Then they add more questions, a questionnaire, a test, extra fields.
You spend your time.
And in return you often get silence, a rejection, or no idea whether anyone saw your application at all.

For me that was the main injustice.

Employers have long had everything: ATS, automatic parsing of applications, automatic candidate search, filters, funnels, AI tools.
In most cases the job seeker still has only hands, eyes and endless manual repetition of the same ritual.

On one side stands a system.
On the other, a person manually proving they deserve to be noticed at all.

To me this seemed a lopsided construction.

If an employer may strengthen themselves with automation, why should a job seeker stay in a pre-digital position and spend hours on something that can be turned into a system?

That irritation is where it all began.

I didn't want to cheat or spam the market.
I wanted to **level the forces** in this endless struggle.

Because in essence the whole application process had long since become the same manual conveyor:
**vacancy → resume → cover letter → questionnaire → submit or a manual dead end.**

At first it was an attempt to get rid of the routine.
Then it grew into a **project** that finds vacancies, picks the right resume, analyses the vacancy text, automatically fills in questionnaires and questions from my real data, and writes each cover letter individually by comparing the vacancy with my resume, experience and portfolio. So it doesn't generate text into the void, it **assembles an application from real matches**.

In the end this is no longer just an application bot but a system that connects search, analysis, resume choice, an individual letter, questionnaire automation and a memory of every action into one managed process.

But at the root of it all wasn't a love of automation.

At the root was **anger at a market** where one side already has the tools and the other still has its time taken by hand.
