---
title: "Voice Reference: how the system knows which voice is mine"
---

**Whisper** can understand what was said.

But by itself it doesn't know who said it.

Even if the system splits a conversation into:

**SPEAKER\_00**
**SPEAKER\_01**
**SPEAKER\_02**

it still doesn't know which of them is me.

For that, **VoxBase** has a separate layer designed for it: **Voice Reference**.

Its job:

**separate the speakers**
**› compare each voice with my reference**
**› label Me / Other**

First I recorded several samples of my voice:

- **iPhone**
- **Mac microphone**
- **different recording conditions**
- **1–3 minutes of speech each**

From these recordings the system creates not text and not an audio copy but a **voice embedding**. It's a numerical fingerprint of a voice.

```
reference = embed(my_voice)

for speaker in speakers:
    score = similarity(embed(speaker), reference)

me = speaker_with_highest_score
```

- **embed()** turns a voice into a set of numerical features
- **similarity()** compares each voice found with my reference
- the voice with the highest match gets the label **Me**
- the rest become **Other**

The full **pipeline** looks like this:

**conversation recording**
**› pyannote splits it by speaker**
**› SpeechBrain creates embeddings**
**› VoxBase compares them with my voice**
**› Whisper adds the text**

The output can then be something other than an impersonal transcript:

```
[00:00:04] Speaker 1: So I want to understand...
[00:00:09] Speaker 2: Yes, let's take a look.
```

Instead, a proper conversation:

```
[00:00:04] Me: So I want to understand...
[00:00:09] Other: Yes, let's take a look.
```

At the same time, **the system shouldn't pretend** to be always sure.

The match **score** is saved in the **metadata**:

- **high › confident match**
- **medium › Me, but with a warning**
- **low › best guess and low confidence**

This is an important **VoxBase** principle: don't hide uncertainty, keep it together with the result.

**Voice Reference** adds a new level.

First **VoxBase** learned to understand:

where there is speech

**Then:**

what was said

**The next step:**

who exactly said it

And here **the transcript starts to preserve the structure of the conversation.**
