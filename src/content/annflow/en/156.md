---
title: "Why voice notes usually die"
---

I've long had a simple but unpleasant problem: after negotiations, meetings and discussions there are too many nuances left that **you can't properly hold in your head**.

During the meeting itself everything seems clear.

- The client clarified something.
- Somewhere the meaning shifted.
- Somewhere an important phrasing came up.
- Somewhere it became clear what the person actually needs.
- Somewhere a small detail appeared that could later grow into a task, a decision or a separate project.

And then the meeting ends.

And the usual **loss of context** begins.

You can turn on the voice recorder on your iPhone.
But an audio file by itself solves almost nothing.
The problem is getting the sound to become working text quickly.

I needed to **get a transcript within about 10 minutes of a conversation**.

**Not through a separate service** that you have to go to, upload a file, wait, check, and download the result.

I needed a **proper path**:

**conversation**
**› recording**
**› transcript**
**›** **text artifact**
**› further work**

The built-in phone solutions didn't work for me.

Quality, stability, control, output format, further processing: all of it quickly **runs into limits**.

A phone is convenient as a recording point, but not as a full speech processing factory.

There is a whole class of devices that record you all day and transcribe automatically. The idea is clear: clip on a small recorder, it listens, then a service turns your day into text. But I didn't want to buy a separate device, pay for a subscription and hand all that logic to an external system.

I decided long ago: **if something becomes part of my memory, my workflow and my personal infrastructure, I want to understand how it works**.

**That's how** **VoxBase** **appeared**.

At first it was the answer to a very practical need: automatically turning iPhone recordings into transcripts.

But it quickly became clear that **the task is broader**.

This isn't only about a voice recorder.

- There are voice notes.
- There are negotiations.
- There's YouTube.
- There's TikTok.
- There are podcasts.
- There are long discussions from which you need to extract meaning, decisions and next steps.

These are all **different sources**, but they share one problem:

important information often arrives as sound or video, while **my working system lives in text**.

**First the recording gets from the iPhone to the Mac.**
**Then the system picks up the file on its own.**
**Then the audio is processed.**
**Then Markdown appears.**
**Then that text can be read, searched, given to an LLM, turned into tasks, posts, notes and decisions.**

That's the first layer.

Other branches grew out of this idea: transcription of **YouTube, TikTok, Apple Podcasts**, and the next project, **LiveContext**, which is no longer about transcribing after a conversation but about **real-time transcription** while the conversation is still going.

But it all started with a simple thing.

I realised that after important conversations I don't need an audio file.

**I need memory**.
