By the same logic I have a second layer on backtick: a tap types the character, and a hold sends the virtual **F17**. I use this layer as an "entry" to an additional command hierarchy through **Leader Key**.

The scheme is this: holding backtick turns on the layer, and from there I can drop into sublayers by meaning.

For example, **backtick (hold) + F** means **Finder** for me. After that the nearest keys on the keyboard become quick shortcuts for opening the folders I need in Finder. This turns navigating the file structure into a couple of presses, with no searching the sidebar and no clicking by hand.

I've made a sublayer for **ChatGPT** in the same way: **backtick (hold) + G**. In this mode the nearest keys open the right project in ChatGPT. In essence it's a "project menu under your fingers" that comes up instantly and works predictably.

The convenience of this second layer is that it doesn't mix everything into one plane: one layer is for everyday navigation, and the backtick layer becomes the entry to deeper, thematic sublayers (folders, projects, tools).
